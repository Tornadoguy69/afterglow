# JWST's open problems, and what we can honestly say about alien life

Research notes for the astronomy site. Same standard as the dark matter piece: every
claim traceable, and a hard line between what is measured and what is argued about.

---

## 1. The Little Red Dots — JWST's biggest genuine mystery

Since 2024 JWST has found **300+** compact, very red objects at redshifts where the
universe was only a few hundred million years old. They were not predicted, and after two
years there is still no agreed explanation. This is the best "open problem" story JWST has.

**What's actually observed:** compact, extremely red sources; often showing broad emission
lines that usually mean gas whipping around a black hole; but *without* the X-ray emission
you would expect from an accreting black hole of that mass. That combination is the
problem.

**Competing explanations, all live as of 2026:**

| Explanation | Idea |
|---|---|
| Direct-collapse black hole seeds | Massive seeds formed early and efficiently, skipping the stellar-remnant route |
| Super-Eddington feeding bursts | Ordinary black holes caught in rare bursts of accretion above the classical limit |
| "Black hole stars" | A black hole cocooned in dense gas, radiating like a giant star |
| Supermassive first-generation stars | Not black holes at all — the universe's first gigantic stars, near collapse |

**A 2026 clue worth building a visual around:** of 83 LRDs studied, **36 had at least one
blue ultraviolet companion — including over 80% of the brightest ones.** That pushes
toward a merger-driven origin.

*Why this is good material:* it is a real, current, unresolved question with a specific
observational signature. Visualising four competing models side by side against the same
data is exactly the format that worked for dark matter.

## 2. Biosignatures — the honest state of play

### K2-18 b — the one everyone heard about

A sub-Neptune 124 light years away. In 2023 and again April 2025, a team reported
dimethyl sulfide (DMS) in its atmosphere — a gas produced on Earth almost entirely by
marine life.

**What happened next is the important part:**

- The claimed detection reached **3σ** at best. Particle physics calls 5σ a discovery;
  3σ means roughly a 1-in-370 chance of arising from noise, and 3σ results fail routinely.
- An independent joint reanalysis of the NIRISS, NIRSpec **and** MIRI data concluded there
  is **insufficient evidence** for DMS or DMDS ([arXiv:2505.13407](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13407)).
- A separate paper is blunt in its title: *K2-18b Does Not Meet The Standards of Evidence
  For Life* ([arXiv:2508.05961](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.05961)).
- A NASA-led study confirmed the planet is water-rich with a complex atmosphere but found
  **no conclusive evidence** of DMS.

DMS also has known abiotic production routes, so even a solid detection would not be proof
of life.

### TRAPPIST-1 e — the quieter, more interesting result

An Earth-sized planet in the habitable zone, 40 light years away. JWST transit
spectroscopy narrowed it to two surviving possibilities:

1. A **secondary, heavy atmosphere** — nitrogen-dominated like Earth's, possibly with
   traces of methane
2. **Bare rock**, no significant atmosphere

Hydrogen-rich atmospheres are **ruled out**. That matters: a small rocky planet keeping a
heavy secondary atmosphere is the actual precondition for habitability, and this would be
the first such detection.

**15 more transits** were observed in December 2025, with results expected in 2026. This is
a live story worth tracking.

Sources: [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/jwst-sees-hints-of-an-atmosphere-on-a-potentially-habitable-exoplanet/) ·
[arXiv:2509.05414](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.05414) ·
[SETI Institute](https://www.seti.org/news/trappist-1-e-revealed-peering-inside-an-exoplanet-s-atmosphere/)

## 3. "Probability of alien life" — the part to get right

**There is no measurable probability of alien life, and any site claiming one is lying.**

This is not pedantry; it is the single most important framing decision for that page.

The Drake equation is often quoted as if it computes an answer:

$$N = R_* \cdot f_p \cdot n_e \cdot f_l \cdot f_i \cdot f_c \cdot L$$

The first three terms are now genuinely measured — star formation rate, the fraction of
stars with planets, the number in habitable zones. Kepler and TESS did that work.

**The last four are unconstrained by any observation.** $f_l$ (fraction of habitable
worlds where life starts) has exactly one data point, and that data point is us, which is
a textbook selection effect: we could only be asking the question on a planet where it
happened. Plug in defensible-looking numbers and the equation yields anything from "we are
alone in the observable universe" to "millions of civilisations in this galaxy."

**What can be stated honestly:**

- We have found thousands of exoplanets and know rocky planets in habitable zones are common
- We have **never** detected a confirmed biosignature
- We have never detected a technosignature
- The Fermi paradox is a genuine puzzle, not an argument for either conclusion

**The good visualisation here is not a probability.** It is the Drake equation itself as an
interactive: sliders for each term, showing that the three we've measured barely move the
answer while the four we're guessing at swing it by 20 orders of magnitude. That teaches
what's actually true — the uncertainty is the finding.

## 4. Confidence tiers for the site

The dark matter piece works because it separates *settled* from *open*. That distinction
has to be structural, not a footnote:

| Tier | Topics |
|---|---|
| **Established** — reproduced, quantitative, not seriously contested | stellar birth and death, nebulae, black holes, planet formation, cosmic expansion, Big Bang back to BBN, dark matter's existence |
| **Active research** — real data, no consensus | little red dots, biosignatures, the Hubble tension, dark energy's nature, dark matter's particle identity |
| **Speculative** — not currently testable | multiverse, pre-Big-Bang cosmology, the anthropic principle |

A visible badge on every page. Putting the multiverse behind the same styling as stellar
fusion would undo the credibility everything else earns.
